Constitution of India

An overview of India's constitutional framework.

The Constitution of India is the foundational legal document that establishes the framework of government, defines powers and responsibilities of institutions, and sets out constitutional rights and principles.


It provides the framework through which the Union and State governments function and establishes principles concerning democracy, justice, liberty and equality.

Fundamental Rights

Major categories of Fundamental Rights under Part III.

Right to Equality

Includes constitutional guarantees relating to equality before law and equal protection of laws.

Right to Freedom

Includes specified freedoms protected by the Constitution, subject to constitutional limitations.

Right against Exploitation

Provides constitutional protections concerning exploitation such as trafficking and forced labour.

Right to Freedom of Religion

Provides constitutional protections concerning freedom of conscience and religion.

Cultural and Educational Rights

Contains protections concerning cultural and educational interests.

Right to Constitutional Remedies

Provides a constitutional mechanism for seeking enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Directive Principles of State Policy

Constitutional principles contained in Part IV.

Social Justice

Principles concerning social and economic welfare and justice.

Economic Welfare

Principles concerning distribution of resources and economic policy.

Public Health

Constitutional guidance concerning public health and related welfare objectives.

Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties are set out in Article 51A.

Fundamental Duties describe constitutional duties expected of citizens. They include respecting the Constitution, national symbols and promoting harmony, among other duties specified in Article 51A.
